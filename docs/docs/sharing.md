# Sharing Posters

Posters.science reduces the process of sharing a FAIR and AI-ready poster to three steps. The goal is to make sharing as fast and painless as possible so that it becomes a natural part of presenting at a conference while still including rich metadata and ensuring readiness for reuse by AI-based workflows.

::: info
You must be signed in to share a poster. See [Creating an Account](./account) for details.
:::

## The three-step workflow

1. **Upload** your poster file. The platform accepts PDFs and images (PNG, JPEG). Drag and drop the file or use the file picker.

2. **Review metadata**. The platform uses AI to read your poster and extract structured metadata: title, authors, affiliations, abstract, funding sources, and more (see [Metadata Extraction](./extraction)). You can easily review the results, correct anything that needs it, and fill in any gaps.

3. **Publish or download**. Publish your poster to Zenodo, where it receives a DOI and becomes permanently citable. The platform generates a `poster.json` file, which is a machine-actionable version of your poster, that also gets archived (see [The Poster Schema](./schema)). Alternatively you can download the poster package (your poster file and `poster.json`) to share it elsewhere.

## How long does it take?

Most posters can be shared in under five minutes.

## What happens after publishing?

Once published, your poster is:

- Assigned a **DOI** that you can include in your CV, grant applications, and publications.
- Stored permanently in a trusted repository (Zenodo).
- Indexed in the Posters.science registry, making it searchable by anyone.
- Described by structured, machine-readable metadata.
